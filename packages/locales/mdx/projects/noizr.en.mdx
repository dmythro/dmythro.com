**Noizr** is a Ukrainian record label and music zine that I run with friends and like-minded people. In 2026 we also opened **Noizr Hub** — a physical record & book store in Podil, Kyiv.

I write the software behind all three. I didn't go with a ready-made platform, because I wanted the store to sell straight from the label's own catalog. So one system runs the website, the store's back office and till, the stickers on the shelves, and the documents a store in Ukraine owes its partners, its accountant and the tax office.

## How it fits together

<NoizrArchitecture locale="en" />

The back office and the till are one application on top of a shared domain core. The public site and the QR pages are separate apps. Each of them connects to the database under its own restricted role and can read only what it shows.

Work that depends on an outside service, such as a fiscal receipt, a bookkeeping entry or an update for a partner, runs as a background job. The job is saved in the same database transaction as the change that needs it, so a sale that was saved has its receipt job saved with it.

The previous noizr.com was built on a PHP CMS I wrote in 2011. For the new version I started again in TypeScript, with Next.js and PostgreSQL. It is a monorepo of several apps and many packages that share the same code, database schema and validation. Most of that code is the store, which the old site never had.

## Why build it instead of buying it

An off-the-shelf e-commerce platform would have been running sooner. Its product model is made for things like clothing, though, and a release has more structure than that. Selling from one would also have meant a second product database beside the label's catalog. And I needed consignment, partner reports and Ukrainian fiscal receipts in the same place.

Noizr is something a few of us do because we want to. I'd like the software to take care of the paperwork, so that our time goes to the music.

The store also needed currency symbols that my [countries-list](/en/projects/countries-list) package didn't have, so I added them there.

## What's inside

### The till

The till handles sales and returns, by card or in cash. I wrote the integration with the bank's payment terminal myself. A payment can't be charged twice, and a payment whose outcome is unknown is never assumed to have failed.

### A sticker and a page for every copy

Every copy in the store has its own code, price and condition. The system lays out the price stickers and prints them on the store's label printer. The QR code on a sticker opens a page for that copy on [go.noizr.com](https://go.noizr.com/), with its price and condition, the other copies and editions on the shelf, and the store's opening hours.

### Partners' stock

Part of the stock belongs to partners and is sold on consignment. Stock is kept in lots, in a ledger that is only ever added to, and each sale records the partner's payout and our commission.

For each contract and period a partner gets an official report, which is numbered, archived as a PDF and mirrored to the accounting system. With one partner the exchange is automatic in both directions. Their whole catalog arrives every night and can be searched from the back office, and our stock levels for their goods are sent back to them whenever they change.

### Money

Every amount is an integer number of kopecks, and every share is in basis points. Splits use largest-remainder allocation, so the parts add up to the total.

The system issues the fiscal receipts the state requires and mirrors every movement of stock and money to the accounting system. The tax return for the third quarter of 2026 was filed from the system's own figures, which matched the books to the kopeck.

### The catalog

A release has editions and pressings, catalog numbers, credits and colors. Those facts come from [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/), and for books from a national library catalog and Google Books. Each source limits how often it can be queried, so background lookups give way to someone scanning a barcode at the counter.

Search works across Cyrillic and Latin and tolerates typos, and an exact barcode or catalog-number match comes first. It runs on PostgreSQL full-text and trigram search.

### The site

The new noizr.com has the zine, the label's releases with our own audio player, and events, in Ukrainian and English. About 2,700 articles and 10,000 images moved from the old CMS into structured data. Before the switch, more than 27,000 old addresses were checked so that every old page opens at its new address.

## Where AI helps

Partners send price lists and invoices as spreadsheets, each in its own layout. AI shapes those rows into our format, and a manager reviews the result before anything is stocked. When stock comes in, it also recognizes an item from a photo of its cover.

During the migration AI sorted and structured the old articles. The cases that needed a judgment call are saved in the repository, so running the migration again gives the same result.

AI agents write much of the code as well, under an architecture and rules I set. Every change passes type checks, tests and an automated review, and I read it before it is merged. The [agent skills](/en/projects/agent-skills) I publish came out of this work.

## Design notes

Before a deploy restarts anything, it pauses new sales and waits for the payments, receipts and print jobs in progress to finish, so it doesn't cut off a customer at the counter.

The ledger is only added to. A return is recorded as the exact reverse of the sale. A price change moves the unsold copies to a new lot, and the sold ones keep the terms they were sold on. A sale can be corrected only until a partner report includes it.

## Status

As of November 2026, Noizr Hub has been open in a soft launch since September and runs on this system. The new noizr.com is live and has replaced the old site. The official opening of the Hub is next, and the online store, selling from the same stock as the counter, will come after that.

The old CMS stays in service for the other sites that run on it.
